# Build: AI-Powered Fashion Purchase Discovery Intelligence Engine

## 1. Role

Act as a senior product engineer, AI engineer, data engineer, and product designer.

I want you to design and build an MVP of an **AI-powered customer discovery and opportunity intelligence engine for Myntra**.

Do not treat this as a sentiment-analysis or review-summarization application.

The purpose of the system is to help a Product Manager understand **why users behave the way they do during fashion-shopping journeys, what prevents them from progressing toward purchase, and which recurring unmet needs could represent meaningful product opportunities.**

---

# 2. Business Problem

Myntra has large amounts of user-generated feedback distributed across:

* App Store reviews
* Google Play reviews
* Reddit
* Fashion/shopping communities
* Public social-media conversations
* YouTube comments
* Product reviews
* Product Q&A
* Other publicly available conversations about fashion and online shopping

This feedback contains valuable signals about user intent, uncertainty, purchase barriers, comparison behavior, and unmet needs.

However, raw feedback is fragmented and difficult for Product Managers to systematically analyze.

Traditional approaches such as:

* Sentiment analysis
* Keyword extraction
* Topic modeling
* Review summaries
* Word clouds

can tell a PM **what users are talking about**, but not sufficiently explain:

> **What is the user trying to accomplish? Why are they behaving this way? What uncertainty or friction prevents progression? How widespread is the problem? Which segments experience it? Could it affect a business metric? And what product opportunity could address it?**

The system should solve this problem.

---

# 3. Primary Business Objective

The initial version should focus on understanding factors that influence:

## Wishlist → Purchase Conversion

Specifically, the system should help answer:

> **Why do users wishlist fashion products but fail to purchase them, and what product opportunities could increase the probability of purchase?**

The architecture should be flexible enough to support other business metrics later, such as:

* Product conversion
* Add-to-cart → purchase conversion
* Repeat purchase
* Search → product-view conversion
* Reduced purchase abandonment
* Engagement
* Customer retention

### External research

* What information do users seek outside Myntra?
* Why do they leave Myntra to find that information?
* Which external sources do they trust?
* What unmet information needs could potentially be solved within Myntra?

* Body type

* Occasion

* Reviews

* Quality

For example:

* Fit uncertainty + return anxiety

# 8. Important Distinction: Observation vs Inference

This is a critical requirement.

The system must distinguish between:

### Observed

Something directly present in the source.

Example:

> User says they are unsure whether the dress will fit.

### Derived

A pattern supported by multiple observations.

Example:

> Fit uncertainty appears frequently among users evaluating dresses.

### Hypothesized

A potential explanation or business implication.

Example:

> Fit uncertainty may contribute to wishlist-to-purchase drop-off.

Never present hypotheses as established facts.

# 9. AI Analysis Pipeline

Build the analysis pipeline in stages.

## Stage 1 — Cleaning

* Remove spam
* Remove duplicates
* Normalize text
* Detect language where relevant
* Preserve original source
* Preserve original context

## Stage 2 — Semantic enrichment

Use Groq LLM to identify:

* Intent
* Journey stage
* Motivation
* Uncertainty
* Barrier
* Decision criteria
* Outcome
* Product/category
* Relevant attributes

## Stage 3 — Semantic clustering

Group semantically similar conversations.

For example:

> "Not sure if this will look good on me."

> "Would this suit my body type?"

> "Wish they showed this on different body shapes."

should potentially cluster under a broader problem such as:

**Appearance / fit confidence**

rather than being treated as three unrelated topics.

## Stage 4 — Pattern detection

Identify recurring patterns across:

* Users
* Products
* Categories
* Sources
* Segments
* Time periods

## Stage 5 — Opportunity generation

Convert recurring patterns into potential unmet needs and product opportunities.

# 10. Opportunity Framework

The central output should be an **Opportunity Map**, not a list of topics.

Represent opportunities as:

**User Segment → Context → Behavior → Uncertainty/Barrier → Evidence → Potential Business Impact → Unmet Need → Opportunity**

Example:

### Opportunity

**Improve fit confidence during product evaluation**

**Segment:** Users considering women's dresses

**Context:** User likes a product but is uncertain about fit.

**Behavior:** User reads multiple reviews and seeks external information.

**Barrier:** Lack of confidence that the product will fit their body type.

**Evidence:** Multiple independent conversations describing fit uncertainty.

**Potential business impact:** May contribute to purchase postponement or abandonment.

**Unmet need:** Personalized fit confidence.

**Potential opportunity:** Help users understand expected fit using richer fit information, contextual reviews, or other product-experience mechanisms.

The system should generate opportunities in this format.

# 11. Opportunity Scoring

Create a configurable opportunity score.

Potential dimensions:

* Reach
* Frequency
* Severity
* Business impact
* Evidence strength
* Cross-source consistency
* Cross-segment relevance
* Trend
* Strategic relevance

Do not assume frequency alone means importance.

For example, a low-frequency issue with extremely high purchase impact may deserve more attention than a high-frequency low-severity complaint.

Show the underlying dimensions separately so PMs can understand why an opportunity ranks highly.

# 22. Important Product Principle

The system should NOT answer:

> "What are users saying?"

It should answer:

> **"What are users trying to accomplish, where are they struggling, why are they struggling, how widespread/severe is the problem, and what product opportunities could potentially address it?"**

The output should therefore move through this hierarchy:

**Raw feedback**

↓

**Behavior**

↓

**Intent / uncertainty / barrier**

↓

**Recurring pattern**

↓

**Unmet need**

↓

**Opportunity hypothesis**

↓

**Potential business impact**

↓

**Evidence required to validate**
